# Strategic Prioritization & PM Judgment

> **Objective:** Separate strategically defensible priorities from features that simply look appropriate for the product.

---

## 1. Feature I Could Not Defend

### Fleet Analytics Manager View

Useful for executives, but it does not directly address the frontline **speed, trust, or workflow issues** driving adoption and churn risk.

**Decision:** ⏸ **Not a 12-week priority.**

---

## 2. Feature With No Strategic Weight

### Contextual AI ETA Display

It sounds relevant for a logistics platform, but the available evidence does not show ETA prediction as a core user problem or a clear retention or revenue driver.

**Decision:** ❌ **Do not prioritize without stronger evidence.**

---

## 3. How My PM Input Improved the AI Output

| Initial AI Output         | PM Input Added           | Result                                 |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Broad feature list        | Fleet Coordinator JTBD   | Focused on frontline operational needs |
| Capability-driven ideas   | UXR evidence             | Priorities tied to real user friction  |
| No clear business filter  | Retention and churn risk | Stronger strategic relevance           |
| Weak prioritization logic | Effort vs. impact        | More defensible roadmap choices        |

**What changed:** The AI did not become smarter. **Better PM context made the output more strategically useful.**

---

## 4. PM Takeaway

AI helped generate and organize options quickly.

My role as the PM was to determine:

* Which problem mattered most
* Which evidence was credible
* Which trade-offs were acceptable
* Which features did **not** deserve investment

> **AI accelerates execution. Product judgment determines direction.**
